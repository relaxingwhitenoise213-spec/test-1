## Media buyer's notes (why the copy is written this way)

1. **Hook before the fold.** Mobile truncates primary text around ~125 characters ("…See more"), so every variation front-loads a scroll-stopping first line.
2. **Personal-attributes compliance.** Meta rejects copy that asserts or implies the viewer's medical/physical state — including pregnancy. That's why CTAs say "moms-to-be" instead of "If you're pregnant, get this ASAP!" (the original's closing line is a common rejection trigger). First-person stories can say "I'm 31 weeks pregnant"; the ad just can't say *you* are.
3. **Relief, not cure.** Claims stay at "designed to relieve pressure / eases / soothes." Never promise elimination of pain or medical outcomes — that's a health-claims rejection and an FTC problem.
4. **Testimonials must be real.** Quotes, names, star counts, and "10,000+ moms" are templates — replace with genuine, typical, permissioned reviews before spending.
5. **Testing plan.** Launch 3–5 primary texts × 2–3 headlines in one campaign using Flexible ad format (or one ad per angle if you want clean reads). Cut anything at ~2× target CPA after enough spend; scale the winning *angle*, then iterate new hooks on it.
6. **Description field is placement-dependent** (it often doesn't render in Reels/Stories) — treat it as a bonus offer/trust line, never load it with essential info.
